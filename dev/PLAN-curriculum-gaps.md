# Curriculum gap check (2026-10-14)

What top universities teach in world history, archaeology and historical sociology/economic history, compared with
what the atlas covers. Raw surveys (course tables with URLs, ranked topics, datasets with licences):
`dev/research/syl_history.md` (14 sources), `syl_archaeology.md` (17), `syl_sociology.md` (12). Many elite syllabi sit
behind logins, so counts are coarse.

## Already strong
Human origins and Out of Africa, Neanderthals/Denisovans, Ice Age climate and land bridges, farming spread, Black Death,
Mongols, Vikings, climate shocks and eruptions, states (Cliopatria), migrations before 1900.

## Most-taught topics we cover least (priority order: how often taught x how well it maps)
1. Cities and urbanisation: city sizes over time as sized points (Reba, Reitsma & Seto 2016: 1,741 cities, 3700 BCE–2000 CE;
   Bosker/Buringh/van Zanden 800–1800; Buringh Europe set CC0). Taught in all three fields.
2. Trade networks: Silk Roads, Indian Ocean (monsoon), trans-Saharan gold/salt, Roman roads (ORBIS/DARMC); route lines with
   active periods + ports. Reuses migration-route drawing.
3. Exploration voyages and maritime empires: Zheng He, Columbus, da Gama, Magellan, Manila galleon; dated tracks.
4. Columbian Exchange: crop/animal/disease transfers with dates (Nunn & Qian 2010); Americas population collapse (debated ranges).
5. Atlantic slave trade: embarkation/disembarkation flows (SlaveVoyages, CC BY-NC); abolition dates. Sensitive: needs care.
6. Domestication centres with first dates per species (Larson et al. 2014) — archaeology's #1 topic.
7. Spread of religions as dated fronts (Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Reformation); reuses SPREADS.
8. Industrial Revolution / Great Divergence: Maddison GDP per head and population (CC BY 4.0), first railways by year, coal.
9. Writing, technology and art firsts (ROAD CC BY-SA for the Palaeolithic; writing systems; metallurgy).
10. Revolutions and independence dates (Atlantic revolutions, 1848, Latin American independence).
11. Ancient DNA ancestry over time (AADR; no licence stated, cite) — strong in archaeology courses.
12. Social history layers: Hajnal line, life expectancy, heights, literacy, real wages (Clio-Infra, licences to check).

## Method explainers worth a small panel or tour
Radiocarbon dating and calibration (IntCal20), which dating method covers which age range, stratigraphy, aDNA, isotopes, LiDAR.

## Tours suggested by the surveys
Silk Roads, Columbian Exchange, Age of Exploration, Bantu expansion, Indo-European languages, spread of world religions,
first cities, Great Divergence.
