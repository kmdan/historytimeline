# Upper-secondary history curricula: Hungary, Poland, Czechia, Italy, Spain

Research date: 2026-10-14. The scope is upper secondary / general academic track (gimnázium, liceum, gymnázium, licei, ESO 3–4 + Bachillerato), ages about 14–19.
Method: official texts where they could be read: the Hungarian kerettanterv (.docx), the Czech RVP G (PDF) and the Spanish BOE decrees (HTML) were read in full. For Poland I read the full annex text of the 2024 core curriculum through a publisher's marked-up copy and checked the citation against the official ELI record. Italy is the weak spot. The 2010 decree on normattiva shows only the annex titles, and the school/publisher copies of the annex returned 404, so the Italian topic list is reconstructed from secondary summaries and from memory of the 2010 text. Treat it as **unverified** until someone checks it against the GU PDF.
Austria and Greece were skipped (optional, out of time budget).

Legend: **[C]** = compulsory/central in the national document. **[N]** = national-specific emphasis. **[EU]** = pan-European or world topic.

## 1. Hungary: NAT 2020 + kerettanterv for gimnázium 9–12

**Sources**
- Framework curriculum, history 9–12 (official, Oktatási Hivatal): https://www.oktatas.hu/kozneveles/kerettantervek/2020_nat/kerettanterv_gimn_9_12_evf (file: https://www.oktatas.hu/pub_bin/dload/kozoktatas/kerettanterv/Tortenelem_K.docx)
- Weekly hours 2/2/3/3 (Eurydice): https://eurydice.eacea.ec.europa.eu/hu/national-education-systems/hungary/magyarorszag-tanitas-es-tanulas-kozepiskolai-oktatasban
- Érettségi changes from 2024 (teachers' association TTE, eduline): https://tte.hu/igy-valtozik-a-tortenelemerettsegi-2024-ben, https://eduline.hu/erettsegi-felveteli/20240319_tortenelemerettsegi-valtozas-2024

**Structure.** There are two chronological passes: grades 5–8 cover everything once, and grades 9–12 cover it again in more depth. Grades 9–10 run from antiquity to 1849; grades 11–12 run from 1849 to today, with 22 hours of exam review. The document says that **about two-thirds of the topic units are Hungarian history** and that world history is treated "like islands" (szigetszerűen). All the content listed (concepts, people, dates, places) is compulsory, and the hours below are the recommended ones.

**Grades 9–10 (topic unit, recommended hours)**
1. Civilisation and the state in antiquity (13 h) [C][EU]: Near East (Old Babylonian state, Hammurabi), Egypt, Athens/democracy, Rome (republic, empire), ancient Israel and the destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE; Pannonia (Aquincum, Savaria).
2. Religions in antiquity (5 h) [EU]: polytheism vs monotheism, Judaism, Christianity.
3. Conquering empires (6 h) [EU, with an N flavour]: **Huns** and the Migration Period, fall of Rome, Byzantium, **Islam and the Arab conquests**.
4. Medieval Europe (10 h) [C][EU]: manor and serfdom, famines and **epidemics**, Church and orders, crusades, towns and guilds, Romanesque/Gothic, Renaissance.
5. Origin of the Hungarians and the Árpád age (14 h) [C][N]: origins (linguistics, archaeology, **genetics**), Etelköz, **Hungarian conquest (honfoglalás)**, raids, Avars, St Stephen, **Mongol invasion 1241**.
6. Heyday of the medieval Kingdom of Hungary (13 h) [N]: Angevins, Hunyadi, Matthias Corvinus, Ottoman threat.
7. Early modern period (12 h) [C][EU]: **voyages of discovery** (Portuguese, Spanish), early colonialism, **world trade**, absolutism, Reformation and Counter-Reformation, Baroque.
8. Ottoman-occupied Hungary (10 h) [C][N, also regional]: Mohács 1526, partition into three, Principality of Transylvania, reconquest of Buda 1686.
9. The Enlightenment (5 h) [EU]: also the American and French revolutions.
10. Hungary in the 18th century (10 h) [N]: Rákóczi's war of independence, resettlement, ethnic change, Maria Theresa, Joseph II.
11. New ideas and industrialisation (5 h) [EU]: liberalism, nationalism, conservatism, **Industrial Revolution**.
12. The Reform Era (10 h) [N].
13. Revolution and war of independence 1848–49 (8 h) [C][N].

**Grades 11–12**
14. Nation states and socialist ideas (6 h) [EU]: Italian and German unification, the USA, Marxism.
15. The Dual Monarchy (15 h) [C][N].
16. The Great War (14 h) [C][EU].
17. Years of transformation (13 h) [C][N]: 1918–19 revolutions, **Trianon 1920**.
18. Between the wars (8 h) [EU]: totalitarianism, Great Depression.
19. Horthy era (12 h) [N].
20. Second World War (18 h) [C][EU]: Holocaust, Hungarian Holocaust.
21. Two world systems (6 h) [EU]: Cold War.
22. From war to revolution (10 h) [N]: 1945–56, Sovietisation.
23. 1956 revolution (7 h) [C][N].
24. Kádár dictatorship (7 h) [N].
25. Bipolar world and its collapse (8 h) [EU]: decolonisation, European integration.
26. Regime change (8 h) [N].
27. The world in the 21st century (6 h) [EU]: globalisation, migration.
28. Hungary in the 21st century (10 h) [N].
29. Hungarians and minorities in the 20th–21st centuries (6 h) [N]: Hungarians beyond the borders.

**Shares (from recommended hours, 275 h of content):**
- 1789/1800 onwards: about 64%.
- 1849 onwards (grades 11–12): about 56%.
- 20th–21st century: about 48%.

**Pre-modern topics:**
- Covered: ancient Near East, Egypt, Greece, Rome, Judaism, Christianity, Huns, Byzantium, Islam, medieval Europe, Mongols, Ottomans.
- **Not in 9–12:** prehistory (őskor). It is taught in grade 5 only.
- Non-European history is almost absent, apart from discoveries, colonialism and decolonisation.

**Érettségi (school-leaving exam):**
- Since 2024 the advanced level adds a list of names, concepts and dates that goes beyond the kerettanterv (criticised by the teachers' association TTE).
- Topics dropped from the advanced level include French absolutism and the 18th-century great-power wars.
- At standard level students write one world-history essay and one Hungarian-history essay.

**Which national emphases are really pan-European:**
- Huns and the Migration Period, the Mongol invasion of 1241 and the Ottoman conquest all belong to Central and Eastern Europe as a region.
- The Habsburg monarchy and 1848 are shared with Austria, Czechia and Poland.
- Trianon is the Hungarian side of the post-1918 treaties (also taught in Czechia, Poland and Italy).
- The Hungarian conquest is purely national, but it is a classic case of steppe migration.

## 2. Poland: core curriculum for liceum/technikum, history (2018, amended 2024)

**Sources**
- Amending regulation of 28 June 2024, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1019, in force since 1 Sept 2024 (official ELI): https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2024/1019/ogl (PDF https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2024/1019/ogl/pol/pdf). It amends the regulation of 30 Jan 2018, Dz.U. 2018 poz. 467.
- Full section text as amended (publisher GWO's marked-up copy; the official one is the annex above): https://gwo.pl/historia-liceum-i-technikum-zmiany-w-podstawie-programowej-2024-w-porownaniu-z-pp-z-2022-r

**Structure.**
- There are 59 numbered sections, taught chronologically over 4 years.
- Each section has a basic (compulsory) level and an extended level.
- The 2024 amendment cut detail ("odchudzanie"), but kept the section list.
- "Historia i teraźniejszość" (HiT, modern history and civics) was dropped for new cohorts from 2024/25.

**Sections (all [C] at basic level)**
- I. History as a science.
- II. **Prehistory** and the ancient East [EU]:
  - Basic: Sumer, Babylon, Egypt, Persia; Jewish history.
  - Extended: prehistory and its ages, **Neolithic farming and sedentism**, states of the **ancient Far East** (China, India).
- III. Ancient Greeks. IV. Rome. V. **Byzantium and the Islamic world** (Arab expansion) [EU].
- VI–VIII. Early medieval Europe, Europe of the crusades, medieval economy and society [EU].
- IX–X, XII. Poland under the early Piasts (966 baptism), fragmentation, 14th–15th c. (union with Lithuania, Grunwald) [N].
- XI. Late medieval Europe (Black Death, Hundred Years' War, Ottomans) [EU]. XIII. Medieval culture.
- XIV. **Voyages of discovery and European colonialism** [EU]. The extended level adds **pre-Columbian civilisations** and the growth of colonial empires in the 16th–18th centuries.
- XV–XVII. Renaissance, Reformation, Europe in the 16th–17th c. [EU].
- XVIII–XXIII. The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth: last Jagiellons, Union of Lublin, free elections, 17th-c. wars with Sweden, Muscovy and the **Ottomans** (Vienna 1683) [N].
- XXIV–XXV. Enlightenment, revolutions of the 18th c. (American, French) [EU].
- XXVI–XXVIII. The Commonwealth in the 18th c., Constitution of 3 May, **partitions of Poland** and the Kościuszko Uprising [N, central].
- XXIX–XXX. Napoleonic era, Europe and the world after Vienna [EU].
- XXXI–XXXII, XXXV–XXXVI. Polish lands 1815–48, January Uprising 1863, life under the partitions [N].
- XXXIII–XXXIV. Europe and the world 1850–1914 (unifications, **imperialism**, industrialisation, new ideologies) [EU].
- XXXVII–XXXVIII. First World War; the Polish question [EU/N].
- XXXIX–XL. After 1918; **totalitarian regimes** [EU].
- XLI–XLIV. Rebirth of Poland, Second Republic (1920 war) [N].
- XLV–XLVII. Road to WWII, defence of Poland 1939, WWII [EU/N].
- XLVIII–L. German and Soviet occupation, **Holocaust / German extermination policy**, Polish Underground State, Katyn [N/EU].
- LI–LIV. Cold War, **decolonisation**, European integration, civilisational change, the turn of the millennium [EU].
- LV–LIX. Communist Poland 1944–89, Solidarity, Third Republic [N].

**Shares (by section count):**
- 19th–21st century: 31 of 59 sections (about 53%).
- 20th–21st century: about 36%.
- Sections that are specifically Polish: about 29 of 59 (about 49%).

**Pre-modern topics:** the broadest of the five countries.
- Prehistory and the Neolithic (extended level).
- Ancient Near East, Greece, Rome, Byzantium, Islam.
- Medieval and early modern Europe, the Ottomans.
- Pre-Columbian America and the ancient Far East (extended level).

**Which national emphases are pan-European:**
- The partitions of Poland are a key case of 18th-century great-power politics.
- The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the 1683 relief of Vienna are East-Central European history.
- Occupation and the Holocaust: the extermination camps are on Polish soil.
- Solidarity and 1989 are part of the fall of communism across the region.

## 3. Czechia: RVP G (framework programme for gymnázia), Dějepis

**Sources**
- RVP G, full text, MŠMT, September 2021 (official, edu.cz): https://www.edu.cz/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/001_RVP_GYM_uplne_zneni.pdf (history = section 5.4.2, pp. 42–45). Decree: https://www.edu.cz/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/001_opatreni_RVP_GYM.pdf. It is binding in all grades by 1 Sept 2025.
- A broader RVP revision is under way (revize.rvp.cz). Its drafts say the new RVPs will drop the "učivo" (content) lists in favour of outcomes: https://revize.rvp.cz/files/04-cas-odpovedi.pdf

**Structure.**
- The national document is short: outcomes plus a list of content (učivo).
- Each school writes its own plan (ŠVP), usually one chronological pass over 4 years, often prehistory → 1914 in years 1–2 and the 20th century in years 3–4.
- The maturita history exam is set by the school, not the state.

**Content blocks (all [C])**
1. Introduction: the work of historians and sources.
2. **Prehistory** [EU]: Stone Age (Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Eneolithic), Bronze and Iron Age. Outcomes:
   - the turning point of farming and crafts ("zásadní zlom");
   - placing the main archaeological cultures in time and space;
   - archaeological cultures on Czech territory.
3. **Antiquity** [EU]:
   - ancient Eastern states: **Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, China**;
   - Greece, Rome, Judaism and Christianity;
   - "our lands in the Roman period": **the limes as a civilisational border**, civilisation vs "barbarians".
4. **Middle Ages** [EU + N]:
   - Byzantine–Slavic sphere, Frankish empire;
   - **Islam and the Arab empire, Mongol and Turkish (Ottoman) expansion**;
   - papacy vs empire, crusades, heresies, **Hussites** [N];
   - **colonisation (Ostsiedlung)**, crafts, trade, **urbanisation**.
5. Early modern period:
   - Renaissance, humanism, Reformation, **overseas voyages**;
   - **Thirty Years' War** [EU, starts in Bohemia 1618];
   - absolutism vs estates, Baroque;
   - the Czech state within the Habsburg monarchy [N].
6. Enlightenment, revolutions, modernisation [EU]:
   - French Revolution, rise of the USA, 1848, Napoleon, Congress of Vienna;
   - **industrialisation** (including its ecological risks);
   - national movements (Czech, Slovak, German, Italian);
   - **imperialism and colonial policy**, rise of Russia.
7. Modern era I, 1914–45: WWI, Russian revolution, Versailles, **Czechoslovakia** [N], Great Depression, Munich 1938 [N/EU], mass culture, WWII (global and economic war, **Holocaust**), Protectorate.
8. Modern era II, after 1945: UN, welfare state, Eastern bloc, Comecon and the Warsaw Pact, NATO and the West, Iron Curtain, **Middle East and Israel**, **decolonisation and the "Third World"**, fall of communism, EU, global problems.

**Shares:**
- From 1789 onwards: 3 of the 7 content blocks.
- In typical school plans this is about 45–50% of hours. This is an estimate: the RVP gives no hours.

**Pre-modern topics:** prehistory (explicit, with archaeological cultures), ancient East including India and China, Greece, Rome and the limes, Byzantium, Islam, Mongols, Ottomans, medieval colonisation and towns, voyages.

**National emphases:**
- The Hussites and the Thirty Years' War (1618) are both Bohemian events with European effects.
- Munich 1938 is pan-European.
- Czechoslovakia 1918–92 is national.

## 4. Italy: Indicazioni nazionali per i licei (DM 211/2010), Storia

**Sources**
- DM 7 Oct 2010 n. 211 (Indicazioni nazionali, licei). The record on normattiva is official, but its page shows only the annex titles: https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/2010/12/14//010G0232/CONSOLIDATED. The full annexes are in GU n. 291 of 14-12-2010, S.O. 275.
- Summary of the chronological ranges (Trento provincial guidelines): https://www.vivoscuola.it/content/download/28801/682802/file/d8d157577dffcd9c9fd06b79af1d1817.pdf
- **New Indicazioni (Valditara, Perla commission):**
  - signed and sent to the CSPI (the national school council, which gives an opinion) in June 2026; expected to apply from 2027.
  - "Storia" and "Geografia" become separate subjects again (they are now taught together as "geostoria").
  - More weight on the West, Europe and Italy; contemporary history reaches the present in year 5.
  - Press reports: https://www.italiaoggi.it/settori/scuola/licei-la-svolta-costituzionale-fevchmy2, https://www.orizzontescuola.it/nuove-indicazioni-licei-storia-e-filosofia-hegel-e-marx-tornano-al-centro-la-contemporaneita-fino-ai-giorni-nostri-entra-in-quinta/

**Structure (2010, in force).** The text is verified for the period structure. The topic list is partly from memory of the official text, so check it before relying on it.
- Years 1–2 (ages 14–16), "Storia e geografia", 99 h/yr together, all [C]:
  - main civilisations of the **ancient Near East**, Jewish civilisation, Greek civilisation, Roman civilisation;
  - the rise of **Christianity**, Roman–barbarian Europe, early medieval society and economy, the early medieval Church;
  - **birth and spread of Islam**, empire and kingdoms in the early Middle Ages (Carolingians), seigneurial and feudal fragmentation.
  - Prehistory is not a named topic here: it is taught in primary school.
- Years 3–4, 66 h/yr, "formation of Europe and its opening to a global dimension, from the year 1000 to the early 1900s":
  - 11th-c. revival; papacy vs empire; **communes** [N, Italy] and monarchies; Church and religious movements;
  - late-medieval society and economy (including the 14th-c. crisis and the Black Death);
  - **Signorie** [N] and territorial monarchies;
  - **geographical discoveries and their consequences**; Reformation; modern states and absolutism;
  - economic development up to the **Industrial Revolution**;
  - English, American and French revolutions; Napoleon and the Restoration;
  - **nationality question, Risorgimento and Italian unification** [N, central];
  - nation states, the social question and labour movement, the second industrial revolution, **imperialism and nationalism**, the Italian state to 1900.
- Year 5, 66 h:
  - mass society, Giolitti era [N], WWI, Russian revolution and the USSR, post-war crisis;
  - **Fascism** [N/EU], 1929 crisis, Nazism, **the Shoah and other 20th-c. genocides**, WWII;
  - Italy from Fascism to the Resistance and the Republic [N];
  - Cold War, **decolonisation and development (Asia, Africa, Latin America)**, Italy after 1945.
- Throughout: non-Western civilisations are to be included (the text names attention to civilisations beyond the West).

**Shares (estimate):**
- 19th–21st century: about 30% of history hours.
- 20th–21st century: about 20%.
- Italy has the most pre-modern weight of the five, because 2 full years are antiquity and the early Middle Ages.

**National emphases:**
- The Risorgimento is the Italian case of 19th-c. national unification, taught in all five countries.
- Communes and signorie are the Italian case of medieval urbanisation.
- Fascism originated in Italy and is pan-European.
- Rome is national and pan-European at once.

## 5. Spain: LOMLOE (ESO RD 217/2022, Bachillerato RD 243/2022)

**Sources**
- ESO (secondary, ages 12–16), RD 217/2022, Annex II "Geografía e Historia" (official BOE): https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-4975
- Bachillerato (ages 16–18), RD 243/2022, Annex II "Historia de España", "Historia del Mundo Contemporáneo" (BOE consolidated): https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-5521 (ELI https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2022/04/05/243/con)
- The regions add their own content: Madrid Decreto 64/2022 (BOCM 26-07-2022): https://www.bocm.es/boletin/CM_Orden_BOCM/2022/07/26/BOCM-20220726-1.PDF. Andalusia: Orden 30-05-2023.

**Structure.**
- The curriculum is organised by competences and themes, not by chronology.
- Each subject has three blocks: A "Sociedades en el tiempo" (societies over time), B "Retos del mundo actual" (challenges of today's world) and C "Compromiso cívico" (civic commitment).
- The state sets only the minimum content; the regions complete it.

**ESO years 1–2 (ages 12–14, just below this report's scope; listed because years 3–4 are almost entirely modern):**
- **origin of humans and of society; the "great human migrations" and the first cultures**;
- **geographical conditions behind the rise of civilisations**; **great trade routes, thalassocracies (sea-based empires) and empires, conquest and colonisation**;
- warfare "from the hoplites to the tercios";
- social hierarchy and power from prehistory to the early modern period; women, slaves and foreigners;
- classical roots, and the **Jewish and Islamic influence on European culture** (al-Andalus implied);
- religions; city and countryside (polis, urbs, villages);
- **voyages, discoveries and exchange in the making of a world economy**;
- **science, medicine and the fight against epidemics and pandemics**.

**ESO years 3–4 (ages 14–16)** [C], almost entirely contemporary:
- from servitude to citizenship: liberal revolutions;
- democracy vs totalitarianism; **capitalism, colonialism and imperialism**;
- conflicts of the first half of the 20th c., **the Holocaust**;
- blocs, **decolonisation**, the new world order;
- Spain and modernity; nationalisms;
- rural exodus and urbanisation, feminism and suffrage;
- European integration; constitutions from 1812 to 1978; democratic memory.

**Bachillerato year 1, "Historia del Mundo Contemporáneo"** (only in the Humanities and Social Sciences track):
- bourgeois and socialist revolutions; parliamentary systems;
- nationalisms; **abolitionism**, civil and social rights; **empires and the national question, liberation movements and decolonisation**;
- growth models and dependency, industrialisation to the post-industrial era;
- demography; the workers' movement; fascism and Nazism;
- **the world wars**, the Spanish Civil War, **the Holocaust and other genocides**;
- League of Nations and UN; transitions to democracy; women's movements;
- globalisation; the multipolar order; **mass migrations (economic, climate, political)**; **the climate emergency**; the EU.

**Bachillerato year 2, "Historia de España"** [C, all tracks; part of the university entrance exam]:
- The state minimum is **contemporary only**:
  - the nation-building process and the liberal state;
  - the Hispanic Monarchy and the colonial legacy, treated as background;
  - Constitution of 1812, Carlism, industrialisation debates;
  - rural vs urban change ("España vaciada", the emptied rural interior);
  - Second Republic, **the 1936 coup, the Civil War and Francoism**;
  - Transition and the Constitution of 1978, terrorism, the EU.
- Earlier periods appear only as long-term themes:
  - the "geostrategic significance of the Iberian peninsula";
  - religion, church and state (including religious minorities).
- The 2022 change of the starting point from prehistory to 1812 was criticised by the Real Academia de la Historia (press, April 2022: https://theobjective.com/espana/2022-04-05/el-gobierno-aprueba-el-nuevo-bachillerato-pasar-de-curso-con-suspensos-y-recorte-a-la-historia/).
- Some regions may have added earlier periods back. I could not verify this for Madrid's annex.

**Shares:**
- Upper secondary (ESO 3–4 + Bachillerato): about 90% or more is 19th–21st century.
- Pre-modern history (prehistory, Rome, al-Andalus, **Reconquista**, the Catholic Monarchs, the Americas) is taught mainly in ESO 1–2 (ages 12–14) and, regionally, in Historia de España.
- So the Reconquista is **not** a national-minimum topic at upper-secondary level.

**National emphases:**
- The Civil War and Francoism are national, but are framed together with European fascism.
- The colonial legacy and the Hispanic Monarchy are global (the Americas, the Philippines).
- Democratic memory is national.
- Al-Andalus and the Reconquista are national with a pan-Mediterranean dimension, but at lower secondary level.

**Overall pattern:**

## Map-friendly topics (gaps in the atlas that have spatial and temporal data)

What the atlas already covers: states (Cliopatria), migrations before 1900, cities, trade routes, voyages, crops, the Black Death, farming spread, climate and ice.

Strong candidates, all taught in 4–5 countries:

1. **Colonial empires 1492–1914, then decolonisation 1945–75.** Each territory has dated start and end years: when it was colonised or became a protectorate, and when it became independent. Cliopatria states partly cover this. An "independence year" layer for the post-1945 world would cover a topic all 5 countries teach. Data: CShapes 2.0 (1886–2019, CC BY-NC). Check the licence before using it.
2. **Spread of the Industrial Revolution.** Railway networks by decade (Europe 1830–1914), coal fields, cotton mills. Possible data: Martí-Henneberg's European railway GIS (check its availability and licence), plus the HYDE urbanisation already used.
3. **The two world wars as moving fronts and occupation zones (1914–18, 1939–45).** Dated front lines are widely published. However, the atlas focuses on the pre-1900 period, so this needs a decision.
4. **Holocaust: camps and deportations.** USHMM and Yad Vashem have dated place lists. This is sensitive and after 1900.
5. **The 1848 revolutions.** About 50 dated city uprisings; a good fit for the existing arrival-front method used for the plague and farming.
6. **Spread of the Reformation (1517–1600).** Dated adoption by territory and city; fits the `SPREADS` front method.
7. **Expansion of Islam (632–750) and of the Ottomans (1300–1683), and the Mongol invasions (1206–1260).** These are dated conquests by city. The states layer shows the result; dated campaign arrows or fronts would show the process.
8. **Crusades (1096–1291)** and **the Migration Period (Huns, Goths, Avars, Magyars, 375–900)**. Route lines with dates fit the migrations layer. The Hungarian conquest (895) is a natural "steppe migration" example; check whether it is already in `migr`.
9. **Atlantic slave trade.** The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (slavevoyages.org) has dated voyages and is linked to abolition. It is pre-1900 and a world topic.
10. **Partitions of Poland (1772/1793/1795), the Congress of Vienna (1815), Italian and German unification (1859–71) and Trianon (1920).** These are border changes the states layer may show only at century resolution. Tour steps could highlight them.

Not map-friendly (better as timeline events only): the Enlightenment, ideologies (liberalism, nationalism, socialism), totalitarianism as a concept, democratic memory, European integration (it could be shown as the EU's year-by-year membership extent, which is easy).
