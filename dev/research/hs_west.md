# Secondary-school history curricula: England, France, Germany, Netherlands

Research date: 2026-10-14. The scope is lower and upper secondary on the academic track (KS3 + GCSE + A level, collège cycle 4 + lycée général, Gymnasium Sek I + Oberstufe, HAVO/VWO), ages about 11–19. Earlier stages are noted where they hold the only pre-modern content.

Method: official texts were read where the site allowed it. Several official hosts refused automated access: education.gouv.fr and eduscol returned 403; lehrplanplus.bayern.de, examenblad.nl syllabus PDFs, aqa.org.uk and edumedia-depot.gei.de refused the connection. Nothing was bypassed. Where the official page was blocked I used:
- official PDFs mirrored by a teachers' union or a school (France première, NRW Sek I), cross-checked against the ministry's structure;
- search-engine extracts of the official pages (Bavaria, AQA, Edexcel, OCR).

**Verification levels:**
- **Read in full:** England KS3; France première (official annex PDF); NRW Oberstufe 2026 (official PDF); Netherlands 49 kenmerkende aspecten (SLO PDF) and the new SLO tijdvakken proposal (PDF).
- **Read as headings:** Baden-Württemberg (official bildungsplaene-bw.de).
- **From a school curriculum that quotes the official plan:** NRW Sek I.
- **From summaries of the official pages:** AQA, Edexcel, OCR, France seconde, collège and terminale, Bavaria, and the Dutch exam contexts.
- Items marked *(unverified)* are from memory of the official texts and should be checked before they are used as facts.

Legend: **[C]** = compulsory/central. **[N]** = national-specific emphasis. **[W]** = European/world topic.

## 1. England

**Sources**
- National Curriculum, history programmes of study KS1–3 (DfE 2013, statutory): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-history-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-history-programmes-of-study
- GCSE history subject content (DfE 2014, binding on all boards): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gcse-history (PDF mirror: https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/19926/1/GCSE_history.pdf)
- AQA GCSE 8145 options leaflet: https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/history/AQA-8145-OE-LEAFLET.PDF; spec: https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/history/specifications/AQA-8145-SP-2016.PDF
- Pearson Edexcel GCSE 1HI0: https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/History/2016/misc/gcse-history-getting-started.pdf
- OCR GCSE History A J410: https://ocr-live-prd95.cambridgeassessment.org.uk/Images/207163-specification-accredited-gcse-history-a-first-teaching-2019-with-first-assessment-2021-j410.pdf; History B (SHP) J411 via ocr.org.uk
- AQA A level 7042 subject content: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/history/a-level/history-7042/specification/subject-content
- Curriculum and Assessment Review final report (Nov 2025, history section pp. 84–87): https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf

**Structure.** History is compulsory to age 14 (KS3). GCSE (ages 14–16) and A level (16–18) are optional, and GCSE History is one of the most-taken optional GCSEs. The KS3 programme lists strands as statutory. **The example topics are non-statutory**, so schools choose. The 2025 Curriculum and Assessment Review proposes only light-touch changes: clearer statutory vs non-statutory content, more diversity and local history, and a review of GCSE content load. It does not change the topic list.

**Pre-modern content sits mostly in KS2 (ages 7–11).** KS2 covers:
- Stone Age to Iron Age Britain (Skara Brae, Stonehenge, hill forts) [C];
- Roman Britain [C];
- Anglo-Saxons, Scots and Vikings [C];
- earliest civilisations: an overview plus one depth study of Sumer, Indus, Egypt or Shang [C];
- ancient Greece [C];
- one non-European society: early Islamic civilisation (Baghdad c. 900), Maya or Benin [C].

**KS3 (ages 11–14), in chronological order.** Strands are statutory and examples are not.
1. Medieval Britain 1066–1509 [C][N]: Norman Conquest, Christendom and the **Crusades**, Magna Carta and Parliament, conquest of Wales and Scotland, feudal society, **Black Death**, Peasants' Revolt, Hundred Years War, Wars of the Roses.
2. Britain 1509–1745 [C][N/W]: **Renaissance and Reformation in Europe**, English Reformation, Elizabethan settlement, **first American colony and first contact with India**, civil wars, Glorious Revolution, Act of Union 1707, Jacobites.
3. Britain 1745–1901 [C]: Enlightenment, **transatlantic slave trade and abolition**, Seven Years War and **American independence**, **French Revolutionary wars**, **Britain as the first industrial nation**, franchise reform, **British Empire** (depth study, e.g. India), Ireland, Darwin.
4. 1901–present [C]: **Holocaust (the only named compulsory item)**, women's suffrage, **WWI** and the peace settlement, Depression and dictators, **WWII**, welfare state, **Indian independence and end of empire**, post-war Britain.
5. Local history study [C].
6. A theme in British history reaching back before 1066 [C], e.g. **migration to, from and within the British Isles**, changing landscape since the Iron Age, or a **turning point such as the Neolithic Revolution**.
7. **One significant society or issue in world history** [C], e.g. **Mughal India, Qing China, the Russian empires**, or the 20th-century USA.

**GCSE rules (DfE 2014).**
- Courses must cover three eras: medieval (500–1500), early modern (1450–1750) and modern (1700–present).
- **British history must be at least 40%** of the course.
- Each course includes one thematic study across all three eras, one period study of at least 50 years, two depth studies from different eras (one British, one European/world), and a historic environment site study.

**GCSE options by board** (schools pick one option per slot):
- **AQA 8145**
  - Period studies: America 1840–95; Germany 1890–1945; Russia 1894–1945; America 1920–73.
  - Wider-world depth studies: conflict and tension 1894–1918, 1918–39, East–West 1945–72, Asia 1950–75, Gulf and Afghanistan 1990–2009 *(list unverified in detail)*.
  - Thematic studies: **health and the people c1000–present**, power and the people c1170–present, **migration, empires and the people c790–present**.
  - British depth studies: Norman England c1066–1100; medieval England under Edward I 1272–1307 *(unverified)*; Elizabethan England 1568–1603; Restoration England 1660–85 *(unverified)*.
- **Edexcel 1HI0**
  - Thematic study plus historic environment: **crime and punishment c1000–present** (Whitechapel); **medicine c1250–present** (Western Front 1914–18); warfare c1250–present (London in WWII); **migrants in Britain c800–present** (Notting Hill).
  - British depth studies: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England 1060–88; Richard I and John; Henry VIII; early Elizabethan England.
  - Period studies: **Spain and the "New World" c1490–1555**; British America 1713–83; **American West c1835–95**; Cold War 1941–91; Middle East 1945–95.
  - Modern depth studies: Russia 1917–41; **Weimar and Nazi Germany** (by far the most common choice); Mao's China; USA 1954–75.
- **OCR B (Schools History Project) J411**
  - Thematic study: the people's health c1250–present.
  - Depth studies: Norman Conquest; Elizabethans; Britain 1900–18.
  - World period and depth studies: **Viking expansion c750–1050**; **First Crusade 1070–1100**; **Aztecs and the Spanish conquest 1519–35**; **Mughal Empire 1526–1707**; Making of America 1789–1900; living under Nazi rule.
- **OCR A J410**
  - Thematic studies: migration to Britain c1000–2010; power: monarchy and democracy; war and British society c790–2010.
  - International relations 1918–75, plus depth studies.

**A level (AQA 7042, the largest board).** Breadth options of about 100 years include:
- Tudors 1485–1603;
- Stuarts;
- Russia in the age of absolutism 1682–1796;
- Tsarist and Communist Russia 1855–1964;
- the British Empire;
- USA;
- Germany;
- Italy;
- Crusades and Byzantium *(unverified)*.

Depth options include Russia 1917–53, Nazi Germany, the English Revolution, the Wars of the Roses, Spain under Philip II, the Cold War and the American civil rights movement. A coursework essay (NEA) is required. No official per-option entry figures were found. Teacher surveys commonly report Tudors, Russia and Germany as most taught (indicative only).

**Share on the 19th/20th century.**
- KS3: about 2 of 7 strands, but these strands carry the only named compulsory item (the Holocaust).
- GCSE: in practice about 55–65%. The 40% British rule and the three-era rule force at least one medieval or early-modern unit.
- A level: heavily early modern and modern. Very little before 1000 CE.

**Prehistory and antiquity at 14–19:** almost none. The exceptions are Norman/Viking/Anglo-Saxon units, long thematic studies starting c790–1000, and OCR's Aztecs, Vikings, Crusades and Mughals. The Neolithic Revolution is only a KS3 example. There is a small separate GCSE/A level in Ancient History (OCR) covering Greece, Persia and Rome, but few schools take it.

## 2. France

**Sources**
- Lycée général programmes: arrêté of 17-1-2019, BO spécial n°1 of 22 January 2019: https://www.education.gouv.fr/bo/19/Special1/MENE1901574A.htm (seconde and première). The terminale programme is in BO spécial n°8 of 25 July 2019. Eduscol portal: https://eduscol.education.gouv.fr/1675/programmes-et-ressources-en-histoire-geographie-voie-gt (403 to automated access; content read via the official première annex mirrored at https://www.snes.edu/wp-content/uploads/hist-geog_annexe-2_1eg_bo.pdf).
- Collège cycles 3 and 4: BO spécial n°11 of 26 November 2015, consolidated BO n°31 of 30 July 2020.
- Draft new collège programmes, CSP (June/July 2025): https://www.education.gouv.fr/le-conseil-superieur-des-programmes/projet-de-programmes-d-histoire-geographie-pour-le-cycle-4-469958. No final arrêté was found as of October 2026.

**Structure.** History and geography is one compulsory subject for every pupil to the baccalauréat. National programmes are fully prescriptive: every chapter is compulsory, with set *points de passage et d'ouverture* (named case studies). There are two chronological passes:
- collège 6e–3e covers antiquity to today;
- lycée seconde–terminale covers antiquity to today again in more depth.

**Collège.** All items are [C].
- **6e (cycle 3, age 11):**
  - the long history of humanity and **migrations**: early humans, the **Neolithic revolution**, first states and first writing;
  - founding stories and citizenship in the ancient Mediterranean: Greek cities, Rome from myth to history, birth of Jewish monotheism;
  - the **Roman Empire** in the ancient world, including its relations with other ancient worlds, **Han China and the Silk Road**.
- **5e:**
  - Christendoms and Islam, 6th–13th century: Byzantium and the Carolingians, from the **birth of Islam** to the **Mongol** capture of Baghdad;
  - feudal society, Church and power, 11th–15th century;
  - Europe and the **opening to the world** in the 16th–17th century: Charles V and Suleiman, humanism and Reformation, the absolute king.
- **4e:**
  - 18th century: **merchant bourgeoisies, international trade and the slave trades**, the Enlightenment, the **French Revolution and Empire**;
  - 19th century: **industrial age**, **colonial conquests**, the Third Republic.
- **3e:**
  - Europe as theatre of total wars 1914–45, including the **Holocaust**;
  - the world since 1945: Cold War, **decolonisation**, European construction;
  - the French Republic rethought.

**Lycée général (ages 15–18).**
- **Seconde, "Major stages in the making of the modern world":**
  - Theme 1, the Mediterranean, legacies of antiquity and the Middle Ages: **Athens (democracy and maritime empire), Rome (Augustus and empire)**; the **medieval Mediterranean as a crossroads of three civilisations** (Latin Christendom, Byzantium, Islam; **Crusades**, trade, Venice) [C][W].
  - Theme 2, the 15th–16th centuries, a new relationship with the world: **Atlantic opening and the consequences of the discovery of the New World**, including the conquest of the Americas, the **slave trade** and the first globalisation; Renaissance, humanism and **religious reformations** [C][W].
  - Theme 3, the state in the early modern period: France and England (Villers-Cotterêts 1539, the British model) [C][N]. One BO adaptation for international sections reads "France et Espagne"; the national text has England.
  - Theme 4, 17th–18th centuries: **Enlightenment and the development of the sciences**; tensions in the society of orders (including the colonial economy and slavery) [C].
- **Première, "Nations, empires, nationalities (1789 to just after WWI)"** (48 h), read in the official annex:
  - Theme 1, Europe faces revolutions: **French Revolution and Empire**; Europe between restoration and revolution 1814–48.
  - Theme 2, France in the Europe of nationalities 1848–71: Second Republic and Second Empire; **industrialisation**; new states built by war (**Italian and German unification**).
  - Theme 3, the Third Republic before 1914: republican project; society; **metropole and colonies**.
  - Theme 4, **WWI**: "the suicide of Europe and the end of the European empires" (world conflagration, societies at war, the peace and the order of nations).
- **Terminale, "Relations between powers and the clash of political models, 1930s to today":**
  - 1929–45: fragile democracies, totalitarianisms, **WWII** and the Holocaust;
  - 1945–early 1970s: bipolar world, **decolonisation and the Third World**;
  - 1970s–1991: oil shocks and the end of the USSR;
  - the world, Europe and France since the 1990s.
- The *spécialité* HGGSP (history, geography, geopolitics and political science) adds thematic, mostly contemporary topics (borders, power, heritage, environment, knowledge).

**Share on the 19th/20th century.**
- Lycée: about 2 of 3 years, i.e. **about 65–70%** (première from 1789 and all of terminale).
- Collège: about 50%.

**Pre-modern content:**
- prehistory and the Neolithic only in 6e;
- Greece, Rome and Judaism in 6e and seconde;
- Byzantium, Islam, the Carolingians, feudalism and the Crusades in 5e and seconde;
- the Mongols in 5e;
- Han China and the Silk Road in 6e.

No ancient Near East beyond "first states, first writing". Non-European history otherwise appears through contact and colonisation.

## 3. Germany (three Länder, Gymnasium)

Education is decided by each Land, but all Länder follow one chronological pass in Sek I (grades 5/6–9/10) and a thematic, mostly modern Oberstufe (grades 11–12/13). History is compulsory throughout Gymnasium Sek I, and in most Länder it is required in the Oberstufe at least to grade 12.

### 3a. Bavaria: LehrplanPLUS Gymnasium (G9)
Source: https://www.lehrplanplus.bayern.de/fachlehrplan/gymnasium/6/geschichte (and /7, /8, /9, /10). The host refused automated access, so the content comes from search extracts of these pages. Each grade has chronological Lernbereiche plus **two Längsschnitte** (long-run themes, e.g. "Wirtschaft und Handel gestern und heute").
- **Gr. 6, "From the first humans to Charlemagne":**
  - humans and their history: **Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers**, the **Neolithic**;
  - **Egypt as an early high culture**;
  - **Greek antiquity**: polis, colonisation, Athens, Persian Wars, Alexander and **Hellenism**;
  - **Imperium Romanum**;
  - from antiquity to the Middle Ages;
  - two long-run themes.
- **Gr. 7, "From the Middle Ages to absolutism":**
  - king and empire (Ottonians), medieval lifeworlds;
  - encounter of cultures: **Crusades**, Constantinople, Islam *(details partly unverified)*;
  - Renaissance and **discoveries**, **Reformation**, Thirty Years War, absolutism *(unverified)*;
  - long-run theme on trade.
- **Gr. 8, "The long 19th century":**
  - Enlightenment, **French Revolution**, Napoleon, Congress of Vienna;
  - **Industrialisation (England as the example)**;
  - nation state;
  - **Bavaria's territorial development from the Middle Ages to 1945** [N];
  - imperialism and WWI *(likely; unverified)*.
- **Gr. 9, "The short 20th century":** Weimar, National Socialism, **WWII and the Holocaust** [C], human rights (long-run theme), Germany and the Allies 1945–49.
- **Gr. 10, "Germany, Europe and the world to the present":** FRG and GDR, 1968, German–German relations, reunification, **European integration to Lisbon 2009**.
- **Oberstufe 11–13:** the new G9 upper school is being phased in from 2025/26; topics not checked.

### 3b. North Rhine-Westphalia: Kernlehrplan Gymnasium Sek I (G9, 2019) and Oberstufe (new, 2026)
Sources:
- Sek I KLP Heft 3407 (2019), via the Lehrplannavigator https://www.lehrplannavigator.nrw.de. Content read from a school's internal curriculum quoting the Inhaltsfelder verbatim: https://www.avhgneuss.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Lehrplan-AvhG-Neuss_Geschichte_G9_Sek1.pdf
- Oberstufe KLP (24 August 2026, in force from 1 August 2027): https://lehrplannavigator.nrw.de/system/files/media/document/file/gost_klp_ge_2026_08_24.pdf

**Sek I Inhaltsfelder** (grade 6 = IF 1–3, grades 7/8 = IF 3–5, grades 9/10 = IF 5–10):
1. Early cultures and first civilisations [C]: **Stone Age ways of life, trade in the Bronze Age, high culture (Egypt)**.
2. Ancient lifeworlds: **Greek poleis** and the **Imperium Romanum** (political participation, everyday life).
3. "What people in antiquity knew about each other": **world views in Africa, Europe and Asia, intercultural contacts** (Herodotus, **Alexander/Hellenism**) [W].
4. Medieval lifeworlds: Frankish Empire and Holy Roman Empire, estates (land, castle, monastery), **towns**, **Christians, Jews and Muslims**, **transcontinental trade between Europe, Asia and Africa**, travellers' views of other cultures [W].
5. Early modern period, "New worlds, new horizons": **Renaissance, humanism, Reformation**, witch persecution and the Thirty Years War, **Europeans and non-Europeans: discoveries and conquests**, **networking and shifting of global trade routes** [W].
6. The long 19th century: **French Revolution** and Congress of Vienna, **1848/49** and German unification, **industrialisation and working life**, the German Empire.
7. **Imperialism (expansion in Africa)** and **WWI**, 1917, the Paris peace treaties.
8. Weimar Republic.
9. National Socialism, **WWII, war of annihilation and Holocaust**, flight and expulsion.
10. International entanglements since 1945 (blocs, proxy wars, **decolonisation**, end of the East–West conflict) and Germany since 1945 *(numbering of IF 9–10 varies in school documents)*.

**Oberstufe, new 2026 KLP** (Inhaltsfelder; Schwerpunkte as listed in the official PDF):
1. Political participation and the striving for freedom (a long-run theme): **Athenian democracy under Pericles**, the late-medieval town, **founding of the USA and abolitionism**, French Revolution, 1848/49.
2. **China and Russia (USSR) in the 20th century**: imperial structures and developments (1911, 1949, 1917; Stalinism, Maoism; "Middle Kingdom" and "russkij mir" world views).
3. Dimensions of modernity: political systems and democratisation, national and global modernisation, **"Europeanisation of the world"** (imperialism), WWI.
4. National Socialism and WWII (Shoah).
5. Post-war order 1945–73, including **independence movements and decolonisation**.
6. Collapse of the post-war order: crises since the 1970s, 1989, European integration, global challenges after 1989.

### 3c. Baden-Württemberg: Bildungsplan 2016 Gymnasium
Source: https://www.bildungsplaene-bw.de/,Lde/Startseite/BP2016BW_ALLG/BP2016BW_ALLG_GYM_G (topic headings read directly).
- **Classes 5/6:**
  - first encounter with history;
  - **Egypt: culture and high culture**;
  - **Greco-Roman antiquity: living together in the polis and the empire**;
  - **from late antiquity into the European Middle Ages: new religions, new empires** (Christianity, **Islam**, Franks).
- **Classes 7/8:**
  - Europe in the Middle Ages: agrarian society and encounters with the foreign (one standard explicitly names **the Silk Road and the Mongol Empire**);
  - turn to modernity: **new worlds, new horizons, new violence** (discoveries, conquest, Reformation);
  - **French Revolution**;
  - Europe after the Revolution: nation state and constitution;
  - industrialised nation state;
  - **imperialism and WWI**;
  - the interwar period.
- **Classes 9/10:**
  - National Socialism and WWII [C];
  - FRG and GDR [C];
  - elective modules marked (*): **"Foreign spaces? Former empires and their present challenges"**, **Russia as a changing empire**, **China as a changing empire**, **Ottoman Empire to secular Turkey**, European integration compared with former empires.
- BW returned to G9 from 2025/26. The plan structure above is the one in force; adjustments for the G9 were not checked.

**Share on the 19th/20th century (Germany).** About 55–60% of Sek I, plus about 85–90% of the Oberstufe. NRW's new Oberstufe keeps only Athens and the medieval town as pre-1500 content.

**Pre-modern content (all three Länder, grades 6–8):**
- Stone Age;
- **Bronze Age trade** (NRW);
- Egypt as the model "high culture";
- Greece and Hellenism, Rome;
- late antiquity, Franks and Holy Roman Empire;
- medieval towns;
- Christians, Jews and Muslims, Crusades;
- **Silk Road and Mongols** (BW);
- **Afro-Eurasian trade** (NRW).

## 4. Netherlands (HAVO/VWO)

**Sources**
- Exam programme (eindtermen) for HAVO and VWO, with the 10 tijdvakken and 49 kenmerkende aspecten: https://examenblad.nl/2020/vwo/documenten/examenprogramma-geschiedenis-vwo
- Full list: SLO, https://www.slo.nl/publish/pages/14265/tijdvakken_en_kenmerkende_asoecten_vwo_bb.pdf (read in full)
- CvTE syllabi: VWO 2025 https://examenblad.nl/2025/vwo/documenten/syllabus-geschiedenis-vwo; HAVO 2027 https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/havo/documenten/syllabus-geschiedenis-havo; 2026 subject page https://examenblad.nl/2026/vwo/vakken/maatschappijvakken/geschiedenis-vwo
- New lower-secondary framework: SLO, *Eindadvies referentiekaders Ruimte en Tijd* + addendum on the core objectives for "mens en maatschappij" (Nov 2025): https://www.slo.nl/publish/pages/18713/eindadvies-referentiekaders-ruimte_en-tijd.pdf

**Structure.** Dutch history is organised by a **frame of reference of 10 eras (tijdvakken) with 49 "characteristic aspects"**. It is used in the lower forms (compulsory for all pupils) and examined in the upper forms (history is an elective exam subject at HAVO/VWO). Since 2025 the 49 aspects are printed in the exam's source booklet. The central exam also tests a few **historical contexts** in depth.

**The 10 tijdvakken and 49 kenmerkende aspecten.** All are compulsory [C] and must be known with an example.
1. **Hunters and farmers (to 3000 BCE):** (1) hunter-gatherer way of life; (2) **origin of agriculture and farming societies**; (3) **first urban communities**.
2. **Greeks and Romans (3000 BCE–500 CE):** (4) Greek scientific thinking and citizenship; (5) classical forms; (6) **growth of the Roman empire spreading Greco-Roman culture**; (7) Greco-Roman vs Germanic culture in NW Europe; (8) Judaism and Christianity as the first monotheisms.
3. **Monks and knights (500–1000):** (9) **spread of Christianity across Europe**; (10) **origin and spread of Islam**; (11) manorialism replaces urban culture in the West; (12) feudalism.
4. **Cities and states (1000–1500):** (13) **rise of trade and crafts, revival of towns**; (14) urban bourgeoisie; (15) church vs state; (16) **expansion of Christendom, including the Crusades**; (17) state formation and centralisation.
5. **Explorers and reformers (1500–1600):** (18) **beginning of European overseas expansion**; (19) Renaissance world view and new science; (20) the classical heritage; (21) **Protestant Reformation**; (22) Dutch Revolt and founding of a Dutch state [N].
6. **Regents and monarchs (1600–1700):** (23) absolutism; (24) the Dutch Republic's Golden Age [N]; (25) **worldwide trade, merchant capitalism and the beginning of a world economy**; (26) Scientific Revolution.
7. **Wigs and revolutions (1700–1800):** (27) Enlightenment; (28) enlightened absolutism; (29) **plantation colonies and the transatlantic slave trade, rise of abolitionism**; (30) **democratic revolutions**.
8. **Citizens and steam engines (1800–1900):** (31) **Industrial Revolution**; (32) the social question; (33) **modern imperialism**; (34) emancipation movements; (35) democratisation; (36) ideologies.
9. **World wars (1900–1950):** (37) propaganda and mass organisation; (38) totalitarianism; (39) crisis of world capitalism; (40) **two world wars**; (41) **genocide, especially of the Jews**; (42) German occupation of the Netherlands [N]; (43) weapons of mass destruction; (44) **resistance against Western imperialism**.
10. **Television and computer (1950–2000):** (45) Cold War; (46) **decolonisation**; (47) European unification; (48) prosperity and socio-cultural change; (49) plural and multicultural societies.

**Exam historical contexts (studied in depth):**
- **VWO 2025** (confirmed in the CvTE syllabus; 2026 assumed the same, PDF blocked): towns and burghers in the Low Countries 1050–1700; **Enlightenment 1650–1900**; **China 1842–2001**; Germany in Europe 1918–1991.
- **HAVO 2025–2027:** **the British Empire 1585–1900**; Germany in Europe 1918–1991; the Netherlands 1948–2008.

**Coming change (lower secondary).** New core objectives for "mens en maatschappij" (definitive draft Nov 2025, awaiting legal adoption) replace the 10 eras with **6 broader periods**: prehistory to 3000 BCE, antiquity, Middle Ages 500–1500, early modern 1500–1800, modern 1800–present. They also set 22 "historical developments", e.g.:
- (1) origin of agriculture;
- (3) **spread of ancient cultures**;
- (4) **spread of the monotheistic religions Judaism, Christianity and Islam**;
- (6) **growth of trade networks and towns**;
- (10) **European colonisation and trafficking of people between continents**;
- (16) nationalism and modern imperialism;
- (19) decolonisation;
- (21) diversity and inequality of wealth;
- (22) information society.

The new framework moves toward global processes and away from Dutch national items.

**Share on the 19th/20th century.** By count, 19 of the 49 aspects (39%) fall after 1800. The exam contexts weigh more heavily on the modern period: about 60–70% of the context material is after 1800, including all of Germany 1918–91 and China. The Netherlands keeps the **most even coverage of the four countries**, with prehistory and antiquity examinable as 8 aspects.

**Pattern:**
- All four countries teach a **national narrative inside a European frame**, with the world entering through contact, expansion, slavery, imperialism and decolonisation.
- **Prehistory and antiquity are taught once, at ages 11–12** (6e, Klasse 6, KS2). Only the Netherlands keeps them examinable up to age 18, as 8 of 49 aspects.
- Weight on the 19th–21st centuries at ages 14–19: England about 55–65%, France about 65–70%, Germany about 80–90% (Oberstufe), Netherlands about 40% by aspect count and higher by exam context.
- The newest revisions all point the same way, toward **long-run global themes**: NRW's 2026 Oberstufe (China, Russia, Europeanisation of the world), the Dutch 2025 core objectives (trade networks, spread of religions, colonisation) and the AQA/Edexcel thematic studies (migration, health).

## Map-friendly (topics with spatial and temporal data the atlas could show)

The atlas already has climate, ice, states, migrations, cities, trade routes, voyages, crops, the Black Death and the farming spread. Taught in all four countries and missing from the atlas:

1. **Atlantic slave trade and abolition** (EN FR DE NL). Data: dated voyages with embarkation and landing regions from SlaveVoyages (CC BY-NC, check); abolition years per country. Shows as flow lines like the migrations layer. Sensitive; needs careful wording.
2. **Spread of the Reformation 1517–1600** (all 4). Dated adoption by city and territory; fits the existing `SPREADS` arrival-front method.
3. **Colonial empires 1492–1914, then decolonisation** (all 4). Each territory has a colonised/protectorate year and an independence year. The states layer partly shows this; an "independence year" layer would cover decolonisation, which all 4 countries teach (but it is post-1945, outside the pre-1900 focus: needs a decision).
4. **Industrial Revolution** (all 4): first railway opening per country or city (Europe 1825–1914), coal fields, cotton towns. Sources need licence checks.
5. **Age of revolutions 1775–1849** (all 4): American, French and Haitian revolutions, Latin American independence and 1848 uprisings as dated points/fronts. 1848 has about 50 dated city uprisings, a good fit for the arrival-front method.
6. **Crusades 1096–1291** (all 4): dated routes (First to Fourth Crusade) as route lines with active years, plus crusader states (in Cliopatria?).
7. **Spread of Christianity and Islam** (FR DE NL, Dutch aspects 9–10): dated conversion/conquest fronts (Islam 632–750; Christianisation of Europe 300–1400).
8. **Medicine and public health** (England's most common thematic study): after the Black Death, cholera pandemics 1817–1860s are dated, mapped outbreaks that reuse the plague method.
9. **Greek colonisation and Alexander's campaign** (DE, FR): dated colonies (Pleiades, CC BY) and a dated campaign route.

Not map-friendly (better as timeline events or panels): feudalism, Renaissance and Enlightenment ideas, absolutism, ideologies, the social question, totalitarianism. WWI/WWII fronts and the Cold War are mappable but post-1900, which conflicts with the current scope.
