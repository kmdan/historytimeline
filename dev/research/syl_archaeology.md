# What universities teach in archaeology / prehistory / human evolution, and what the atlas could show

Researched 2026-10-14 (web; no Reddit). Many syllabus pages return 403/404 or are unreadable PDFs (UCL module catalogue, Harvard, Durham, Michigan, CSU, Rutgers), so some rows rest on official catalogue descriptions or search-result excerpts. Rows marked (desc) have no weekly list. Notes on the brief: "Human Evolution: Past and Future" is a **UW–Madison** MOOC (John Hawks), not an Edinburgh one. No Leiden prehistory MOOC turned up; Leiden's MOOC in this area is *Osteoarchaeology*.

## 2. Most recurring topics (count = how many of the 17 rows cover it; approximate)

Legend: **[have]** = already in the atlas (per CLAUDE.md), **[gap]** = candidate addition.

1. **Origins of agriculture and domestication (~13).** Sub-themes: Natufian and pre-pottery Neolithic; multiple independent centres (Fertile Crescent, China, New Guinea, Mesoamerica, Andes, E North America, Sahel/Ethiopia); domestication syndrome; why people farmed (debated); pastoralism. Map/timeline: [have] farming-spread tour layer. [gap] **domestication centres with first-date per species** (wheat, barley, rice, millet, maize, squash, potato, goat, sheep, cattle, pig, dog, horse, chicken, llama) from Larson et al. 2014 PNAS / Zeder; crop arrival isochrones per crop (e.g. maize northward, rice into SE Asia, the Bantu crop package); later "Neolithic farmer" aDNA ancestry.
2. **Early hominins and the genus *Homo* (~10).** Miocene apes, Sahelanthropus, Australopithecus, Paranthropus, *H. habilis/erectus*, first dispersal out of Africa (Dmanisi ~1.8 Ma), Middle Pleistocene *Homo*. Atlas starts at 500 ka, so: [have] hominin finds after 500 ka; [gap] a **pre-500 ka "deep prologue"** (key fossil sites with dates, Oldowan 2.6 Ma, Acheulean 1.76 Ma, Lomekwi 3.3 Ma, debated), or at least a fossil-site card set.
3. **Origin of *H. sapiens* and Out of Africa (~9).** Jebel Irhoud ~300 ka, pan-African origin; early exits (Misliya, Apidima, debated); main dispersal ~60–50 ka; Sahul by ≥50 ka (Madjedbebe 65 ka debated). [have] hominins and migrations; [gap] **ancestry split dates from genetics** (Basal Eurasian, ~45 ka Ust'-Ishim, Bacho Kiro), a "first evidence per region" layer.
4. **Methods: dating and chronology (~9).** See §3.
5. **Cities, states, urbanism (~8).** Uruk, Egypt, Indus, Erlitou/Shang, Teotihuacan, Maya, Andes; why states form. [have] Cliopatria states; [gap] **city points with population over time** (Reba, Reitsma & Seto 2016 "6,000 years of urbanization"; Modelski; Chandler), first-writing systems with dates.
6. **Neanderthals and Denisovans, admixture (~7).** Range, extinction ~40 ka, interbreeding pulses, Denisova and Baishiya Karst, Vindija. [have] hominin finds; [gap] **admixture events as timeline items** (~47 ka Neanderthal pulse, Denisovan pulses in Sahul/E Asia) and the % archaic DNA in living populations by region.
7. **Bioarchaeology: diet, isotopes, disease, osteology (~6).** Shift to farming and health; stature; dairy and lactase persistence; mobility from strontium. [gap] **isotope points** (IsoArcH): C/N diet, Sr mobility; lactase persistence spread.
8. **Ancient DNA and population history (~6).** Steppe ancestry ~3000 BCE, Anatolian farmers to Europe, Bantu expansion, Austronesians, First Americans, Out of Africa bottleneck. [gap] **aDNA sample points (AADR) coloured by ancestry component over time**: probably the most "modern-syllabus" layer not yet in the atlas.
9. **Hunter-gatherers and non-farming paths (~6).** Mesolithic (Star Carr), Jōmon, Aboriginal Australia, NW Coast complex foragers, ethnographic comparison. [have] c14 sites; [gap] forager vs farmer share over time (HYDE land use), Jōmon/NW Coast as tour.
10. **Technology: stone tools to metallurgy (~6).** Oldowan, Acheulean, Levallois/MSA, blades, microliths, pottery (Jōmon/Xianrendong ~18 ka), copper (~5000 BCE), bronze, iron. [gap] **"first appearance" layer per technology**, with ROAD giving Palaeolithic industries.
11. **Peopling of the Americas (~5).** Beringia, coastal vs ice-free corridor, White Sands footprints (~21–23 ka, debated), Monte Verde, Clovis. [have] ice, sea level, migrations; [gap] **ice-free corridor opening date**, key-site cards with "debated" flags.
12. **Upper Palaeolithic and symbolic behaviour / art (~5).** Blombos ochre, Sulawesi and Chauvet art, ornaments, burial. [gap] **rock art and ornament sites with dates** (oldest dated per region).
13. **Mortuary archaeology and burial (~5).** Sima de los Huesos (debated), Qafzeh, Sungir, megaliths, royal tombs. [gap] burial/monument site points.
14. **Social inequality and complexity (~5).** Ranked societies, chiefdoms, elites. [gap] Seshat variables (social complexity index per polity per century).
15. **Survey, remote sensing, GIS (~5).** See §3.
16. **Heritage, ethics, who owns the past (~5).** Repatriation, colonialism, community archaeology. This works as an "about the evidence" panel more than a layer.
17. **Palaeoenvironment and humans in extreme environments (~5).** Deserts, high altitude (Tibet, Andes), rainforest, islands, Arctic. [have] climate, biomes, ice; [gap] "first occupation" dates for Tibet Plateau, Arctic, remote islands.
18. **Mesopotamia and Egypt (~5).** Ubaid, Uruk, Ur, dynastic Egypt. [have] events/eras; Pleiades would give places.
19. **Bronze/Iron Age Europe and Mediterranean (~4).** Steppe migration, Aegean palaces, Late Bronze Age collapse (~1177 BCE), Rome. [have] states; [gap] DARE/Pleiades Roman roads and towns, Late Bronze Age collapse tour.
20. **Oceania and Pacific settlement (~4).** Sahul, Lapita ~3000–2800 BP, Polynesia to New Zealand ~1250–1300 CE. [gap] **island first-settlement dates** (Wilmshurst et al. 2011 chronology) as an expanding-front layer, same model as farming spread.
21. **Mesoamerica and Andes (~4).** Olmec, Maya and the Classic collapse, Teotihuacan, Inka; maize domestication. [gap] LiDAR-revealed Maya settlement as a method story.
22. **Africa (~4).** MSA, African Humid Period, Bantu expansion, Sahel states, Great Zimbabwe. [have] AHP in biomes; [gap] Bantu expansion front (aDNA + language).
23. **Empires and long-distance trade (~3).** Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, medieval globe. [gap] ORBIS-like route network, trade-good finds.
24. **Collapse and resilience (~3).** Classic Maya, Late Bronze Age, Akkad 4.2 ka event (debated). [have] climate events; link climate events to collapses, marked "debated".
25. **Ongoing and recent human evolution, gene-culture coevolution (~3).** Lactase persistence, malaria resistance, high-altitude adaptation (EPAS1 from Denisovans), skin pigmentation. [gap] allele-spread cards tied to farming, pastoralism and altitude.
26. **Disease and epidemics (~3).** Plague aDNA (*Y. pestis* in Neolithic/Bronze Age, Justinianic, Black Death), malaria. [have] Black Death 1346–53; [gap] earlier plague aDNA finds (Gok4 ~2900 BCE etc.), Justinianic plague.
27. Primates/Miocene apes and evolutionary theory (~3). Out of time range; possibly a short intro panel.

## Bottom line for the atlas

Already strong on the deep-time environmental frame (climate, ice, sea level, biomes), hominins, megafauna, 14C, farming spread and states. The most-taught topics still missing are:
- (a) domestication centres per species;
- (b) aDNA ancestry through time (AADR);
- (c) first-appearance dates for technologies, art and writing;
- (d) cities with population (Reba 2016);
- (e) Pacific/island and Bantu expansion fronts (same machinery as farming spread);
- (f) explainers for radiocarbon calibration and the "which dating method" bar.
