# Upper-secondary history: Nordic, Ireland, IB, European Schools, Council of Europe

Research date 2026-10-14. Scope: what students aged about 14–19 are taught in history, so the atlas can add missing topics.
Official texts were used where they could be read (Finland, Sweden, Norway, Ireland, European Schools). The IB and Council of Europe sites block automated access (Cloudflare challenge / HTTP 403). They were skipped, and the IB lists below come from the published guide structure as summarised by third-party revision sites. They are marked as such.

Legend: **C** = compulsory, **O** = optional / elective. "Share 19th/20th c." is my estimate of the share of prescribed content after 1800, judged from the wording. It is not an official figure.

---

## 1. Finland – lukio, LOPS 2019 (in force from 2021)

Source: Opetushallitus, *Lukion opetussuunnitelman perusteet 2019*, §6.13 Historia, pp. 283–289 — https://www.oph.fi/sites/default/files/documents/lukion_opetussuunnitelman_perusteet_2019.pdf (also on https://eperusteet.opintopolku.fi).

| Module | C/O | Core content (keskeiset sisällöt, condensed) |
|---|---|---|
| **HI1 Ihminen, ympäristö ja historia** (Humans, environment and history), 2 cr | C | History as a discipline and periodisation. **Agriculture as the basis of societies**: humans reshaping the environment, farming, division of labour and "birth of culture", **population growth, formation of societies and states**, money economy and trade. **Birth of world trade**: Europeans exploring and conquering the world, diversification of trade and its social/environmental effects. **Industrialisation**: preconditions, social and environmental effects, **population development and migrations**, post-industrial society and global economy. Explicitly "European and global perspective", "from the beginning of agriculture to today", with statistics literacy. |
| **HI2 Kansainväliset suhteet** (International relations), 2 cr | C | Late 1800s to today: ideologies; **imperialism**; **causes and consequences of the world wars**; democracy vs totalitarianism; human rights, **the Holocaust and other genocides**; **Cold War** and its end; **decolonisation**; the changing balance of world power. |
| **HI3 Itsenäisen Suomen historia** (Independent Finland), 2 cr | C | The legacy of Swedish rule and autonomy under Russia; demographic change and population diversity; modernisation; independence (1917), **civil war (1918)** in international context; democracy crisis of the 1930s; Finland in WWII; Cold War effects; welfare state; a culturally diversifying Finland. Mainly 1860s–today. |
| **HI4 Eurooppalainen ihminen** (The European human), 2 cr | O (national elective) | Worldviews from **antiquity** to today: ancient cultures; **birth of democracy and scientific thinking**; medieval culture and **interaction of cultural spheres**; early-modern science and art; **Reformation** and the "information revolution" (printing); **Enlightenment**, human rights and equality; 19th-c. ideologies; popular culture, globalisation, gender roles. |
| **HI5 Ruotsin itämaasta Suomeksi** (From Sweden's eastern land to Finland), 2 cr | O | Finland as part of the Baltic region **from prehistory to autonomy**: research methods and sources of prehistory, **ideas about the origins of Finland's population groups**; medieval state formation around the Baltic, church and secular power; Reformation, Swedish great-power era, estate and farming society; union with Russia (1809). Sámi and minority heritage are named in the objectives. |
| **HI6 Maailman kulttuurit kohtaavat** (World cultures meet), 2 cr | O | The concept of culture, and encounters between Western and other cultures across history. Two or more of: **Asian, African, Arctic, Australian/Oceanian Indigenous, Latin American, Middle Eastern, North American Indigenous cultures**. |

- **Pre-modern coverage:** compulsory only as a long thematic arc in HI1 (farming → states → trade → world trade). Antiquity, the Middle Ages, and Finland's prehistory and medieval history are optional (HI4, HI5). HI6 is the only module with a non-European cultures focus, and it is optional.
- **Share 19th/20th c.:** about 75–80% of compulsory content (HI2 and HI3 are almost entirely after 1860; HI1 is roughly half modern).
- **Atlas relevance:** HI1 matches the atlas's long view very well (farming origins, state formation, world trade, voyages, industrialisation, migrations, environment).

## 2. Sweden – gymnasieskolan, Historia (Gy25: SKOLFS 2023:130; earlier Gy11)

Sources: Skolverket, current subject syllabus HIST (SKOLFS 2023:130, Gy25, in force from 1 July 2025) — https://syllabuswebb.skolverket.se/subject/HIST/1/pdf. Gy11 version for comparison (Skolverket appendix 11, 2015 revision draft) — https://www.skolverket.se/download/18.6bfaca41169863e6a65c342/1553966898658/Bilaga%2011:%20%C3%84mnesplan%20f%C3%B6r%20%C3%A4mnet%20historia.pdf

Structure: 1a1 + 1a2 (50 + 50 points) or 1b (100 points) is the common core (1b on university-preparatory programmes, 1a1 on vocational ones). 2a (general), 2b (culture) and 3 are deeper levels (programme-specific or electives).

| Level | C/O | Historical content (condensed, Gy25) |
|---|---|---|
| **1a1** | C (vocational) | European periodisation (**prehistory/"forntiden", antiquity, Middle Ages, early modern, modern**) in chronological overview. **Industrialisation and the successes and setbacks of democratisation worldwide from the Enlightenment to today**. Central global processes, e.g. **development of agriculture**, technology, changing resource use, **migration, colonialism and liberation, genocide**, conflicts, international cooperation. **National minorities' roots, including the Indigenous Sámi**; racism against the Sámi, antisemitism, antiziganism. |
| **1a2** | C (vocational) | Long historical lines (living conditions and power); problematising periodisation; industrialisation and democratisation in Sweden from the Enlightenment, compared with other countries. |
| **1b** | C (university-prep) | All of 1a1 plus the long lines of 1a2 and Sweden compared with other countries. Gy11 wording also listed population development, **state formation, development of agriculture**, and comparison with a non-European periodisation (e.g. China, India). |
| **2a** | O / programme | Intellectual and cultural currents in some epochs (science, politics); industrialisation, democratisation and other global processes; minorities in Sweden or elsewhere; idealist vs materialist explanation models. |
| **2b – kultur** | O / programme | The same, through art, literature and film. |
| **3** | O | Ideas about gender, minorities, children and human worth across epochs; **an in-depth study of one central global change process or event**. |

- **Pre-modern coverage:** compulsory, but only as a chronological overview of the European epochs plus long lines (agriculture, state formation). No prescribed ancient or medieval case study.
- **Share 19th/20th c.:** about 50–60% of the compulsory courses (industrialisation and democratisation from about 1750, plus 20th-century genocide and conflicts). It is higher in practice, because the overview of earlier epochs is brief.
- **Distinctive:** strong emphasis on **historiebruk** (uses of history), Sámi, Jewish and Roma history, and comparing periodisations across cultures. A world map with a changing timeline directly supports the "epochs" and "periodisation" items.

## 3. Norway – Historie, fellesfag studieforberedende (LK20, HIS01-03)

Source: Utdanningsdirektoratet, Læreplan i historie (HIS01-03), competence aims after Vg2 — https://www.udir.no/lk20/his01-03/kompetansemaal-og-vurdering/kv84 — and after Vg3 — https://www.udir.no/lk20/his01-03/kompetansemaal-og-vurdering/kv85
Compulsory common subject on the general-studies track (Vg2 and Vg3; roughly 56 + 113 teaching hours; check the hours against Udir's subject and hour distribution).

| Level | C/O | Competence aims (condensed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Vg2** | C | Periodisation and its criteria; local and national remembrance; **important changes in how people have obtained food and used natural resources** (sustainability); **demographic changes**, their causes and effects; **how trade and economic systems affected power and people's lives**; **ancient vs modern democracy**; **communication and cultural encounters** in Norway and the world; **power from the Middle Ages through the early modern period**; **religion and power from the Middle Ages to today**. |
| **Vg3** | C | Interpretation and uses of history; a historical person; continuity and change; **ideologies and political revolutions from the Enlightenment**; **technological revolutions from the Industrial Revolution**; **causes of migration and the resulting cultural encounters**; **why colonial powers took territories and the consequences for the colonised**; **background of the world wars** and other key conflicts, and peace settlements; people's choices in conflict; **20th-century ideologies → oppression, terror, genocide such as the Holocaust**; national identity construction in Norway; **empowerment and liberation in Norwegian and Sámi history**; the welfare state; **humans and nature, resource use**. |

- **Pre-modern coverage:** Vg2 is thematic and long-run (food and resources, demography, trade, Athenian democracy, medieval power and religion). It is the most pre-modern of the Nordic cores.
- **Share 19th/20th c.:** Vg2 about 30–40%, Vg3 about 80%. Overall roughly 60%.
- **Atlas relevance:** Vg2's "food and natural resources", "demography", "trade and economic systems" and "cultural encounters" map almost one-to-one onto the atlas's crops, HYDE population, trade routes and migrations layers.

## 3b. Denmark (stx) – brief note only

The official stx curriculum (Historie A, 2017, uvm.dk) could not be downloaded (old PDF links return 404). According to secondary descriptions, the core content (kernestof) requires the main lines of Danish, European and world history **from antiquity to today**, forms of government and social organisation, **state and nation formation** (including Denmark), and the points of the **Danish history canon (historiekanon, 29 points)**. Examples I believe are on it (check against the canon list) include the Jelling stones, the Kalmar Union, the Reformation, absolutism 1660, the 1788 abolition of adscription, the 1849 constitution, 1864, women's suffrage 1915, 1943 and the fall of the Wall 1989. Guidance 2024: https://www.uvm.dk (Vejledning Historie A stx 2024). Not counted in the ranking below.

## 4. Ireland

### 4a. Junior Cycle History (ages ~12–15; specification 2017)
Source: NCCA, *Junior Cycle History Specification* — https://www.curriculumonline.ie/getmedia/34acdfbe-fcbf-47c2-a7ea-1e430df58e06/Junior-Cycle-History-Specification.pdf
All learning outcomes are compulsory, and many give the student a choice of example.

- **Strand 2, Ireland:** settlement and **plantation** (e.g. growth of towns); two parliamentary leaders; a pre-20th-century rebellion; nationalism and unionism 1911–23; the Troubles; **Christianity in Early Christian Ireland**; **the Great Famine and the Irish diaspora**; Irish people in WWI or WWII; women in the 20th century; one sporting or cultural movement; local history; the 1960s; Ireland's links with Europe.
- **Strand 3, Europe and the wider world:** **one ancient or medieval civilisation** (student's choice); **conquest and colonisation, especially Portuguese and Spanish exploration**; **one pre-20th-century revolution**; WWI or WWII; the **Cold War**; **life and death in medieval times**; the **Renaissance**; the **Reformation** and one reformer; one fascist and one communist country; **genocide, including the Holocaust**; **technology and innovation**; the EU or UN; the 1960s; a long-run theme (**crime and punishment, food and drink, work and leisure, fashion, health and medicine**).
- **Pre-modern:** about one third of the outcomes (one ancient or medieval civilisation, medieval life, Renaissance, Reformation, exploration, Early Christian Ireland, plantations).

### 4b. Leaving Certificate History (ages ~16–18; current syllabus 2004, revision due 2027)
Source: *Leaving Certificate History Syllabus* (DES/NCCA) — https://www.curriculumonline.ie/getmedia/da556505-f5fb-4921-869f-e0983fd80e50/SCSEC20_History_syllabus_eng.pdf

Students take **one field** and study **four topics** (2 Irish + 2 Europe/world, one of them a prescribed documents-based topic), plus a research study (20%).

- **Early Modern 1492–1815, Irish:** Tudor reform and Reformation; Elizabethan rebellion and conquest; 1603–60; colonial ascendancy 1660–1715; 1715–70; the Union 1770–1815.
- **Early Modern, Europe/world:** Renaissance to Reformation 1492–1567; religion and power 1567–1609; eclipse of Old Europe 1609–60; Louis XIV 1660–1715; **establishing empires 1715–75**; **empires in revolution 1775–1815**.
- **Later Modern 1815–1993, Irish:** Ireland and the Union 1815–70; reform movements 1870–1914; sovereignty and partition 1912–49; **the Irish diaspora 1840–1966**; Northern Ireland 1949–93; the Republic 1949–89.
- **Later Modern, Europe/world:** **nationalism and state formation 1815–71**; nation states and tensions 1871–1920; dictatorship and democracy 1920–45; division and realignment in Europe 1945–92; **European retreat from empire 1945–90**; the USA and the world 1945–89.
- **Share 19th/20th c.:** schools report that more than 95% of candidates take Later Modern (school website claim, not official), so in practice almost 100% is post-1815.

### 4c. Draft new Leaving Certificate History (consultation 2026, from Sept 2027)
Source: NCCA draft specification — https://curriculumonline.ie/getmedia/dfe88b3d-9079-4cf9-b668-14664ac495fc/SC-History-ENG-INT.pdf ; NCCA page https://ncca.ie/en/senior-cycle/curriculum-developments/history
Four strands: 1 Historical inquiry; 2 **Source investigations: four prescribed investigations, one each from medieval, early modern, 19th century and 20th century onwards** (half Irish, half world; topics set by the Department); 3 **Ireland 1879–2009** (choose 1 of 3 options); 4 **The World 1917–2009** (choose 1 of 3: 1917–45, 1945–79, 1979–2009). The appendices mention, among others, the **Great Chinese Famine** and **pollution and the climate crisis**.
**Trend:** the draft moves the main content even later (from 1879 and 1917), but restores a little pre-modern history through the source investigations.

## 5. IB Diploma Programme History (very common in international schools in Europe)

The official ibo.org pages are behind a Cloudflare challenge and were skipped. The structure below follows the IB *History guide* (first assessment 2017, last exams 2027), as widely reproduced (e.g. https://www.activehistory.co.uk/ibdp). Details of the new course come from third-party sites (https://www.revisionvillage.com/ib-history/hl-2028/ , https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-syllabus-changes-tracker) and must be checked against the IB subject brief.

**Current course (last exams 2027).** SL = Paper 1 + Paper 2 + internal assessment; HL adds Paper 3.
- **Paper 1, prescribed subjects (choose 1 of 5, C):** 1 Military leaders (**Genghis Khan c.1200–27**, Richard I 1173–99); 2 **Conquest and its impact** (end of Muslim rule in Spain 1482–92; **conquest of Mexico and Peru 1519–51**); 3 The move to global war (Japan 1931–41, Italy/Germany 1933–40); 4 Rights and protest (US civil rights 1954–65, apartheid South Africa 1948–64); 5 Conflict and intervention (**Rwanda 1990–98**, Kosovo 1989–2002).
- **Paper 2, world history topics (choose 2 of 12):** 1 Society and economy 750–1400; 2 Causes and effects of medieval wars 750–1500; 3 Dynasties and rulers 750–1500; 4 Societies in transition 1400–1700; 5 Early modern states 1450–1789; 6 Early modern wars 1500–1750; 7 **Industrialisation 1750–2005**; 8 **Independence movements 1800–2000**; 9 Democratic states 1848–2000; 10 Authoritarian states (20th c.); 11 Causes and effects of 20th-century wars; 12 The Cold War. **Every topic must be studied with examples from more than one region of the world** (a key difference from the national curricula).
- **Paper 3 (HL), one regional option of 18 sections each:** Africa & Middle East, Americas, Asia & Oceania, Europe. They range from about 750 CE to the 2000s (e.g. Abbasid and Fatimid states, trade and the spread of Islam in Africa, **indigenous societies of the Americas c.750–1500**, **the Silk Road**, Japan under the shoguns, medieval England and France, and the Ottoman and Mughal empires in the early modern sections).
- **Pre-modern:** fully available (750–1789), but in practice most schools teach the 20th-century options (move to global war, authoritarian states, Cold War, 20th-century wars). This is a widely reported observation, not an IB statistic.
- **Earliest content:** 750 CE. Nothing on prehistory or antiquity.

**New course (first exams May 2028):** prescribed subjects become **focused studies** (choose 1 of 5 paired studies; titles reported as *Climate and innovation; Independence and identity; Political and economic transitions; Conflict and displacement; Protest and change*, unverified), world history topics become **thematic studies** (choose 1 of 4, e.g. "Conflict"), and HL keeps **regional studies** (2 of 12 per region). Less content, more depth. A climate theme is new.

Sources (official syllabi archived by the Georg Eckert Institute, originally on eursc.eu):
- S4–S5 History syllabus, ref. 2005-D-3610-en-4, in force Sept 2006 — https://edumedia-depot.gei.de/bitstream/handle/11163/2442/798028734_2006_A.pdf?sequence=2
- S6–S7 History syllabus, approved Feb 2013 (S6 from 2013, S7 from 2014) — https://edumedia-depot.gei.de/bitstream/handle/11163/2423/798027827_2013_A.pdf?sequence=2

| Years (age) | C/O | Content |
|---|---|---|

| **S6–S7 (16–18), 2-period (C if not 4-period) and 4-period (O)** | Core C | **Compulsory core "Europe and Europeans since 1914"**: WWI; dictatorship and democracy 1918–39; Europe in WWII; post-war Europe 1945–49; East/West 1949–73; dictatorship to democracy 1974–95; Europe in the making (1945–today). **Themes**: S6 (choose ≥3): USA 1898–1945, culture and society before 1945, **European colonialism and imperialism from the 19th c. to 1945**, warfare in the 20th c., women in the 20th c., Russia/USSR 1917–53. - **Pre-modern:** S4–S5 only (medieval to about 1870). S6–S7 is 100% after about 1898.
- **Distinctive:** a deliberately *European* rather than national core, with reference points spread across countries (e.g. Finnish independence 1917, Portugal's Estado Novo 1933, the Dutch famine 1944).

## 7. Council of Europe and EUROCLIO (cross-European recommendations)

The coe.int / rm.coe.int pages returned HTTP 403 to automated requests and were skipped. The points below come from search-result summaries of the official pages.
- **Recommendation Rec(2001)15** on history teaching in 21st-century Europe (Committee of Ministers, 31 Oct 2001): covers the aims of history teaching, the **European dimension**, syllabus content, methods, ICT, and **the misuse of history**. It came out of the 1997–2001 project "Learning and teaching about the history of Europe in the 20th century". It stresses **multiperspectivity**, sensitive and controversial issues, and remembering crimes against humanity. Later instruments: intercultural dialogue in history teaching (2011), **Roma and Traveller history** (2020), **Holocaust remembrance** (2022), and the Observatory on History Teaching in Europe (OHTE, 2020). https://www.coe.int/web/history-teaching/list-of-recommendations
- **"Shared histories for a Europe without dividing lines"** (2010–14 e-book) has four shared themes: **the Industrial Revolution**, **development of education**, **human rights as reflected in art**, and **Europe and the world** (interaction, exchange, encounters). https://www.coe.int/en/web/learning-resources/-/shared-histories-for-a-europe-without-dividing-lines
- **OHTE General Report on the State of History Teaching in Europe** (Dec 2023, 16 member states plus Ukraine): the main problems are **overloaded curricula and too little time**. A related comparative study notes that national history still dominates but European and world history are growing. https://www.coe.int/web/observatory-history-teaching/general-report
- **OHTE thematic report "Pandemics and natural disasters as reflected in history teaching"** (2022/23): despite their impact, **pandemics and natural disasters get little attention in history curricula**. It also warns about scapegoating in crises. This is directly relevant to the atlas's Black Death, eruptions and climate layers.
- **EUROCLIO / Historiana** (https://historiana.eu, a JavaScript app that was not readable): source collections and "key moments" focus on **WWI, WWII, the Cold War, post-war Europe**, women at work, railways and connectivity, and religious art. It works as a multiperspective "alternative to a European textbook".

## Map-friendly

Topics with good spatial and temporal data for a world map plus timeline (and how they relate to existing layers):
- **Farming origins → states → trade** (FI HI1, NO Vg2, SE): already covered by crops, farming spread, Cliopatria states, cities and trade routes. A tour titled like the FI HI1 arc ("from farming to world trade to industry") would match three curricula directly.
- **Age of exploration and conquest of the Americas** (all 6): voyages layer exists. Possible additions: Columbian Exchange crops (the crops layer could show journeys after 1492), and population collapse in the Americas (HYDE).
- **Colonial empires and decolonisation** (all 6): Cliopatria states give the empires. Independence dates per country (1776–1990s) suit a choropleth "year of independence" layer. Decolonisation lies mostly after 1900, but these are state changes, not migrations, so they would not break the pre-1900 migrations focus.
- **Industrialisation spread** (all 6): railway network growth (e.g. country railway km by year, datable lines), coalfields, and first factories and cities. City growth from 1800 is already in the cities layer.
- **19th-century migrations and diasporas** (Irish Famine 1845–52, Nordic emigration, Atlantic slave trade): fit the pre-1900 migrations scope. The Irish Famine and diaspora are compulsory in Ireland. The Atlantic slave trade has well-sourced voyage data (SlaveVoyages; check its licence).
- **Revolutions and democratisation**: map of revolutions 1775–1848 and suffrage dates (male and female) per country, a simple choropleth by year.
- **Reformation**: confessional map (1517–1648) spreading from Wittenberg, Zürich and Geneva. It is in IE, SE, NO, ES, FI-O and IB.
- **Mongol conquests (Genghis Khan, IB), Silk Road (IB HL)**: the trade routes layer and Cliopatria already partly cover these.
- **World wars, Cold War blocs, the Holocaust**: very strongly taught. They are spatial (fronts, alliances, camps) but sit outside the atlas's current deep-time and pre-1900 emphasis. This is a scope decision for the user, and the Holocaust needs care.
- **Indigenous peoples** (Sámi, Arctic, Indigenous Americas and Australia: FI HI6, SE, NO): traditional areas could be shown with "peoples" labels (historical-basemaps peoples already exist).
- **Pandemics and natural disasters**: the CoE says they are under-taught. The atlas's Black Death and eruptions layers are a distinctive strength. Possible additions: the 1918 influenza or cholera pandemics of the 1800s (pre-1900 fits).
