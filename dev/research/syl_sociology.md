# Historical / comparative sociology and economic history syllabi: topics and datapoints for a map-timeline

Research date: 2026-10-14. Sources are web pages and syllabus PDFs (no Reddit). Many top-school sociology syllabi sit behind Canvas logins (Harvard, Princeton, Chicago, Columbia), so where no public weekly list exists the table uses the public course description and says so. The economic-history courses (Berkeley, Davis, MIT, LSE, Oxford) are the closest public material for long-run social change.

## 2. Recurring topics (count = courses out of 12 where the topic has a week or a named unit)

1. **States, empires and state capacity (10/12).** Covers state origins, city-states vs territorial states, "war made the state" (Tilly), the fiscal-military state, bureaucracy and empires.
   - *Map-timeline datapoints:* polity extent and capital; type (city-state, territorial state, empire); Seshat "social complexity" measures (polity population, territory, hierarchy levels, bureaucracy, writing, money, taxes); fiscal capacity (tax revenue per head, from the Global Price and Income History Group and Karaman & Pamuk for Europe 1500-1800); army sizes.
   - *Datasets:* Cliopatria (already in the atlas), Seshat Equinox / Polaris2025, Brecke Conflict Catalog 1400+, Correlates of War (1816+).

2. **Growth, industrialisation and the Great Divergence (8/12).** Covers the Malthusian trap and its escape, the Industrial Revolution, catch-up, and Europe vs China, India and Japan.
   - *Datapoints:* GDP per head by country and region from AD 1 (Maddison 2023); real wages of building labourers (Allen); spread of railways, steam and cotton mills by country and date of first adoption.

3. **Population and Malthus to the demographic transition (7/12).** Covers mortality crises, the European marriage pattern, the fertility decline and population cycles.
   - *Datapoints:* population density (HYDE, already present); life expectancy (Clio-Infra, Human Mortality Database: Sweden from 1751, France from 1816); crude birth and death rates; date the fertility decline began by country or province (Princeton European Fertility Project, Coale & Watkins 1986); famines with death tolls.

4. **Inequality, class and stratification (7/12).** Covers social order, polarisation, "survival of the richest", the Great Leveler (war, revolution, collapse, plague) and social mobility.
   - *Datapoints:* Gini estimates (Clio-Infra income Gini 1820+; Milanovic, Lindert & Williamson "ancient inequality" points for Rome 14 CE, Byzantium 1000, England 1290/1688, Mughal India 1750, etc.); house-size Gini of Neolithic and Bronze Age sites (Kohler et al. 2017, *Nature*, 63 sites); top income shares (WID, from about 1870 for some countries); wage-to-wealth ratios after the Black Death.

5. **Slavery, serfdom and unfree labour (6/12).** Covers the Atlantic slave trade, plantation economies, serfdom (including the second serfdom in eastern Europe), abolition dates and the demography of slavery.
   - *Datapoints:* voyages and embarkation/disembarkation flows by region and decade, with an estimated 12.5 m embarked 1501-1866 (SlaveVoyages); Indian Ocean and Saharan trades (estimates only, mark them as such); dates of abolition of serfdom and slavery by country (Wikipedia-checkable, e.g. Russia 1861, Brazil 1888).

6. **Colonialism, European expansion and globalisation (6/12).** Covers the Columbian exchange, trading companies, extractive institutions and the 19th-century global economy.
   - *Datapoints:* colonial possessions over time (Cliopatria and historical-basemaps); trade-route volumes (VOC voyages: Dutch Asiatic Shipping 1595-1795, Huygens Institute; Manila galleons); migration of Europeans and Asians (indentured labour) 1820-1914.

7. **Religion and society (5/12).** Covers Weber's Protestant ethic, Durkheim, the Reformation and confessional states, the Axial Age (Bellah), and the spread of world religions (UW course "expansion of world religions 500-1700").
   - *Datapoints:* majority religion by region over time; spread fronts of Buddhism, Christianity and Islam; Reformation adoption by territory and date; witch trials (Leeson & Russ dataset, about 43,000 trials 1300-1850 in 21 European countries); monasteries and missions.
   - *Datasets:* Database of Religious History (UBC, https://religiondatabase.org); Seshat religion variables.

8. **Cities and urbanisation (5/12).** Covers city-states, urban systems, "cities and growth", and urbanisation rates as a proxy for GDP (Acemoglu, Johnson & Robinson; de Vries).
   - *Datapoints:* city sizes over time. Reba, Reitsma & Seto 2016 has 1,741 cities and 10,353 city/date/population values, 3700 BCE-2000 CE. Bosker, Buringh & van Zanden cover European, Middle Eastern and North African cities 700-2000. de Vries covers European cities over 10,000 people, 1500-1800 (R package `europop`). Hanson 2016 covers Roman cities. Also urbanisation rate by region.

9. **Revolutions, rebellion and protest (5/12).** Covers social revolutions (Skocpol), popular protest, peasant revolts and civil wars.
   - *Datapoints:* revolts and revolutions as dated events with locations (Brecke catalogue; Seshat crisis database; the Chinese peasant-revolt series); waves (1640s "general crisis", 1789-1848, 1848).

10. **Institutions: property rights, guilds, money and finance (5/12).** Covers parliaments, guilds, banking, sovereign debt and the Gold Standard.
    - *Datapoints:* parliament activity per century (van Zanden, Buringh & Bosker 2012); bullion flows (Potosí silver output); coin debasements; adoption of the Gold Standard by year.

11. **Family, household and marriage (4/12).** Covers stem vs nuclear households, the Hajnal line, age at marriage and women's work.
    - *Datapoints:* the Hajnal line (St Petersburg-Trieste) as a map overlay, about 1750-1900; mean age at first marriage; household types (Ethnographic Atlas via D-PLACE, CC BY, about 1,300 societies with family, kinship and subsistence variables).

12. **Nation-states and nationalism (4/12).** Covers Gellner and Anderson ("print capitalism"), and nation-building after 1789.
    - *Datapoints:* dates of independence and unification (Italy 1861, Germany 1871, Latin America 1810s-20s); spread of printing presses (Dittmar 2011: European cities with presses by 1500); vernacular newspapers.

13. **Agricultural origins and agrarian society (4/12).** Covers the Neolithic, the "Neolithic mortality crisis", manorialism and agricultural revolutions.
    - *Datapoints:* farming arrival front (already in the atlas); domestication centres; land use (HYDE cropland and pasture); field systems; potato and maize spread after 1492.

14. **Epidemics and mortality crises (4/12).** Covers the Black Death, depopulation of the Americas after contact, and plague as a "leveler".
    - *Datapoints:* Black Death (already present); Justinianic Plague 541-549; New World epidemics (smallpox 1518-20, cocoliztli 1545 and 1576); 1918 influenza for context; cholera pandemics 1817-1923.

15. **Living standards and the body (3/12).** Covers real wages, heights and nutrition (Steckel), and the welfare ratio.
    - *Datapoints:* welfare ratios by city (Allen; Global Price and Income History Group, gpih.ucdavis.edu); average heights by country and decade (Clio-Infra, from about 1810; Koepke & Baten for Europe AD 1-1800).

16. **Literacy, education and print (3/12).**
    - *Datapoints:* literacy rates (Clio-Infra / van Zanden et al. *How Was Life?*, 1820+); book production per century (Buringh & van Zanden 2009, Europe 500-1800); universities by founding date; printing presses by 1500.

17. **Race, ethnicity and the colour line (2/12).** Covers Du Bois and the African-American family.
    - *Datapoints:* slave and free populations in US censuses (IPUMS from 1850); colonial racial categories. Present cautiously as history, not as essentialised categories.

18. **Gender and women's work (2/12).**
    - *Datapoints:* female age at marriage; women's suffrage dates (New Zealand 1893 is the earliest national case); women's wage ratios (sparse).

19. **Environment and resources (2/12).** Covers "nature" (Oxford), Diamond's geography argument, and coal (Pomeranz).
    - *Datapoints:* coalfields and their opening dates; deforestation (HYDE, KK10); climate shocks (already present: eruptions, LIA).

20. **Crime, law and witchcraft (1/12, but a recurring social-history staple).**
    - *Datapoints:* witch-trial counts by region and decade (Leeson & Russ 2018); homicide rates, Europe 1300-1900 (Eisner 2003, 5 regions, sharp decline: a robust long-run series).

Gap: mass migration (1820-1914, 50+ million Europeans out; Indian and Chinese indenture) is taught mainly in globalisation units (LSE, Princeton edX) rather than as its own week. It fits the atlas's existing migrations focus before 1900.

## Notes for the atlas
- Already covered: population density (HYDE), states (Cliopatria), migrations, farming spread, Black Death, climate.
- Highest-value additions, by how often the topic recurs and how easily it can be mapped:
  - **City sizes over time** (Reba et al. as point symbols sized by population; Bosker et al. for 800-1800 Europe and the Middle East)
  - **GDP per head / real wages** (Maddison, Allen) as a region choropleth or popup sparkline
  - **Atlantic slave trade flows** (SlaveVoyages, as a flow layer like the migrations; non-commercial licence)
  - **Polity complexity / state capacity** (Seshat, in popups on states; non-commercial licence)
  - **Demographic markers**: the Hajnal line, fertility-decline onset, life expectancy
  - **Inequality points**: Gini estimates as popup facts, marked "estimate"
  - **Religion and Reformation spread** as dated fronts, similar to the plague and farming layers
